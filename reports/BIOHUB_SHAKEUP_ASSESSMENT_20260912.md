# Biohub: public 0.966 versus OOF 0.68152

Assessment on 2026-09-12: a fall from 0.966 to approximately 0.68 is possible, but there is no defensible probability estimate or evidence that this is the expected private result. These numbers concern different models/protocols and different data. EXP209's 0.6815218332750073 is a reciprocal cross-fit reference, not a calibration point for every high-LB solution or the unvalidated unknown-embryo production selector.

## Mechanisms

The official metric is adjusted edge Jaccard plus 0.1 times division Jaccard. Edges require both endpoints to be matched within 7 micrometres. Missed cells, merged detections and displacement therefore damage association as well as detection. Movie weights are TP+FP+FN; a large difficult movie can matter more than many small easy ones. The node-count adjustment also depends on predicted counts relative to estimated true counts. Changes in embryo morphology, density, signal quality, motion or annotation/count-estimate composition can consequently change performance and ranking. This is an inference from the [official metric](https://github.com/royerlab/kaggle-cell-tracking-competition/blob/main/metrics.md), not a claim about the undisclosed private composition.

Unlike squared-error RMSE, there is no unbounded quadratic contribution from one very large coordinate residual. The spatial matching threshold can nevertheless turn localization errors into missed nodes and edges. A 0.286 drop cannot be explained solely by losing the division term, whose entire contribution is at most 0.1: at least 0.186 must come from the adjusted edge component if those are the final scores. Public-specific threshold/pruning tuning and selecting the best of many LB trials can amplify a change in domain mixture.

The [official data description](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/biohub-cell-tracking-during-development/data) confirms embryo-disjoint train/test and that visible example test samples are training copies replaced by hidden data during submission. It says the hidden test is approximately training-set size. It does not disclose the exact number of public movies or public/private embryo identities. Four visible Zarr examples are not evidence of a four-movie public LB. Historical four-movie-bootstrap intervals in OOF_STABILITY.md and ROGII_COMPARISON.md remain conditional simulations; their interpretation as the actual public sample size is withdrawn.

## What our evidence supports

EXP209 achieves 0.6658905036 forward and 0.7716450342 reverse, a 0.1057545306 difference across the two held-out embryo directions. This demonstrates material domain sensitivity in this model family, but does not estimate the private-score drop for a 0.966 model. Only two biological domains underpin the 175 movies. Movie bootstraps quantify conditional movie uncertainty and paired deltas; they do not establish generalization uncertainty over unseen embryos. The development-adapted experiment family also means repeated OOF-based model selection must not be confused with a pristine final external test.

A large rank shake-up can occur with a much smaller absolute score change when teams are tightly clustered. Conversely, a broad score shift can leave ordering fairly stable. The operational decision is to obtain EXP212's own LB result with the exact frozen model policy before further OOF improvement. It will provide the first actual OOF-to-LB comparison for this production attempt, while the unknown-embryo selector remains a separate validation gap. No new OOF experiment was started for this assessment.
