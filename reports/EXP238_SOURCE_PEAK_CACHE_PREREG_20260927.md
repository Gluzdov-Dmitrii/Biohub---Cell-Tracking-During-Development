## Fixed hypothesis and decision

On **each** of the two inner-validation source cohorts (eight `44b6` movies and eleven `6bba` movies), at least 40% of endpoint-limited false-negative edges have an off-graph local maximum within the official 7 µm match radius for **every** unmatched endpoint. The same 40% gate must hold when distinct unmatched GT endpoint nodes, rather than edges, are the denominator. All four rates (two cohorts × two denominators) must pass. Otherwise **reject track-seeded redetection** as the next intervention. This test measures only the existence of a detector proposal; it does not establish a feasible graph repair or an OOF improvement.

The pool of denominator edges is fixed by the verified source-inner decomposition: EXP227 has 153 endpoint-limited FN edges and EXP236 has 514. A distinct endpoint node is counted once even when incident to multiple FN edges. For an edge with two unmatched endpoints, both require qualifying peaks. Numerators and denominators must be reported explicitly; an empty denominator fails closed. No threshold, distance, endpoint definition, movie subset, or gate may be chosen after source-label inspection.

## Integrity, bounds, and stop rules

The derived runner must verify the two original graph-code manifests, original graph `status.json` hashes, exact 19 graph CSV and per-movie receipt hashes, plan/checkpoint hashes, and all 19 source image IDs before model inference. It must write into new `exp238_...` code/run namespaces, never alter the frozen parent trees. Replay each graph through the derived code and require its CSV SHA256 to equal the sealed parent; on any mismatch, stop before source-label access. Output only sparse binary peaks and per-movie metadata, no full logits or probability volumes. Cap at 16,384 peaks per frame, 1,000,000 per movie, and 228,000,000 payload bytes overall; exceeding a cap fails closed rather than truncating peaks. Atomic files, hashes, exact record counts, finite scores, coordinate bounds, and one frame-metadata row per frame are mandatory.

All 19 movies have 100 frames of raw shape `(64,256,256)`. The pinned `(1,4,4)` downsample produces one `(64,64,64)` logit volume per frame: 262,144 voxels = 1 MiB float32. Caching all 1,900 frames would cost 1,992,294,400 bytes (1.855 GiB) float32 or 996,147,200 bytes (0.928 GiB) float16, before container overhead. The requested sparse record is 12 bytes (`uint16` t/z/y/x and float32 probability), bounded by 12,000,000 bytes per movie and 228,000,000 bytes across 19; a separate small frame manifest stores branch/kernel details. The existing graph runs took 694.893 s for source8 and 723.349 s for source11 on one A100 with eight CPUs; budget approximately 24 A100 minutes plus peak extraction/I/O overhead, with separate fair queue leases and bounded worker time. This is an estimate, not a launched job.
